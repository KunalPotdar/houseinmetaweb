Buying an Apartment Before It's Built? 7 Things You Should Check Before You Decide

By HouseInMeta · Published 26 November 2026

Your future home deserves more than a floor plan. Discover what to look for before investing in an apartment that hasn't been built yet.

Buying a new apartment before construction can be an exciting opportunity. You may choose your preferred apartment, floor, location and sometimes even interior finishes before your future home takes shape.

But how can you be confident about a home you haven't physically experienced? Architectural floor plans, brochures and 3D exterior images are useful, yet they may not answer how the home will feel in everyday life. Before making one of life's biggest investments, consider how the layout, space, movement and natural light will work for you.

Checklist for your next apartment
  1. Understand the complete layout
  2. Look beyond square metres
  3. Understand movement through the home
  4. Consider light, orientation and views
  5. Evaluate the balcony and outdoor space
  6. Explore finishes and customization
  7. Check technical details

1. Understand the complete apartment layout

A floor plan shows bedrooms, bathrooms, the kitchen, living areas and corridors, but interpreting a 2D drawing can be difficult. Before deciding, understand how rooms connect, where the entrance and circulation areas are, how the kitchen relates to dining and living spaces, whether bedrooms offer enough privacy and how the balcony connects to the interior.

Why it matters: a well-understood layout helps you evaluate whether the apartment suits your family's everyday needs. Interactive 3D floor plans can provide perspectives that a flat drawing cannot.

2. Look beyond square metres: understand the actual space

An apartment advertised as 80 m² may look spacious on paper, but its usable comfort depends on the arrangement of walls, furniture, doors and corridors. Check whether the sofa and dining table fit comfortably, bedroom furniture can be arranged conveniently, corridors are practical and enough storage is available. Review room dimensions and ceiling heights rather than the total area alone.

Why it matters: Two homes with the same floor area can feel very different. A 3D apartment model helps visualize room proportions, furniture arrangements and the relationship between spaces.

3. Understand how you will move through your home

Consider how easily you can move between rooms, navigate corridors and reach the balcony. Can you move around furniture without feeling restricted? Does the layout support your normal daily routine?

A walkthrough or 3D circulation view can make movement from bedrooms and corridors to living areas and balconies easier to understand. This is a useful complement to the dimensions in the official plan.

4. Consider natural light, orientation and views

Ask which direction the main windows face and how sunlight will reach each room. Consider the balcony orientation, surrounding buildings, possible future construction, privacy and likely noise from traffic, lifts, refuse areas or neighbouring units.

Check whether the apartment can receive cross-ventilation and ask about the building's energy performance. Building models can illustrate context, but actual orientation, daylight and views depend on the final design and surroundings, so verify them with the developer.

5. Evaluate the balcony and outdoor space

Before purchasing, confirm that the balcony is accessible from the living room and has enough usable space for outdoor furniture or your intended use. Ask about approximate usable area, views, privacy and the relationship between indoor and outdoor spaces.

A 3D visualization can make the balcony's size and connection to the home easier to assess, but it does not replace the exact dimensions and contractual inclusions.

6. Explore interior finishes and customization options

Depending on the developer and construction stage, you may be able to select flooring, kitchen finishes, wall colours, bathroom materials or other options. An interactive configurator can help compare combinations more clearly than separate samples.

Always confirm which options are available under your purchase agreement. A preview should not be treated as a guarantee of texture, appearance or included content unless that is written into the contract.

7. Check the technical details and what is included

Review the official apartment plan, area calculation method, construction specifications, fittings, parking, storage, common facilities and expected delivery date. Also check for differences between illustrative images and final specifications, permitted area tolerances and any price adjustment if the delivered apartment is smaller.

  • Which materials or designs may the developer substitute, and is your approval required?
  • What structural and defects warranties apply?
  • How will defects be reported and corrected at handover?

Ask the developer to clarify any unclear clause before signing the purchase agreement.
