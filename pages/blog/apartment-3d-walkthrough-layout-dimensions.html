BUYER GUIDE · APARTMENT WALKTHROUGHS

What can you experience in a 3D walkthrough of your apartment?

By HouseInMeta · Published 27 November 2026

A 2D drawing can tell you the dimensions of your apartment, but numbers alone do not tell the whole story. What will the rooms feel like? How will they connect? And will your furniture fit comfortably?

A 3D walkthrough brings the layout off the page, allowing you to explore the apartment from a human perspective and understand how its dimensions translate into everyday space.

In this guide
  1. The challenge of 2D drawings
  2. Follow the layout
  3. Get a feel for room dimensions
  4. Imagine furniture and circulation
  5. Test everyday routines
  6. Compare apartments and verify details

Why dimensions on a 2D drawing do not tell the whole story

A floor plan is essential for understanding an apartment, but reading it requires you to translate lines, symbols and measurements into a space you have never visited. A room's area does not reveal its shape at a glance, and a doorway or window can change how much usable space remains along a wall.

It can be difficult to picture the distance between rooms, the width of a corridor or the space left after placing a sofa. Two apartments with similar floor areas can feel very different depending on their proportions and layouts. This is especially challenging when buying off-plan, before you can visit the finished home.

How a 3D walkthrough helps

A 3D walkthrough turns the plan into a space you can explore at eye level. Moving through it helps you connect measurements with room proportions, see how spaces relate to one another and imagine your daily routine. The drawing still provides the measurements; the walkthrough gives those numbers context.

Follow the layout from room to room

Start at the entrance and walk through the apartment as you would on a normal day. Where do you arrive? Can you see directly into the living room? How far is the kitchen from the dining area, and are the bedrooms separated from the busiest spaces?

Moving between rooms makes the relationships shown on a plan easier to picture. You can notice a long corridor, a bathroom near a bedroom or a kitchen that opens into the living area. These details affect privacy, convenience and how the home feels as a whole.

Get a feel for room dimensions

A bedroom listed as 12 square metres can be arranged in many ways. A long, narrow room and a nearly square room may have the same area but offer different options for a bed, wardrobe and desk. In a walkthrough, look at the length and width together, as well as the positions of doors and windows.

Stand in each room and look towards the corners. Notice ceiling height, alcoves, columns and wall space where these are represented. The view can help you connect the numbers on a plan with the proportions of the space around you.

Imagine furniture and space to move

Furniture gives dimensions context. In the living room, consider whether a sofa and dining table leave a comfortable route to the balcony. In the bedroom, imagine walking around the bed and opening the wardrobe. In the kitchen, look at the space between counters and the path to the dining area.

Furniture shown in a model may be illustrative. Compare its dimensions with your own furniture and check clearance for doors, drawers and chairs against the measured plan. If the experience offers measurement or furniture tools, check how they work and what project data they use.

Test your everyday routines

Picture bringing groceries from the entrance to the kitchen, working from home while someone relaxes in the living room, or getting ready in the morning. Is there a practical place for a desk? Can family members reach the bathroom without crossing a bedroom? Is storage close to where you need it?

Look at the direction doors open and how circulation crosses furnished areas. A room can have a generous area on paper yet have limited usable wall space because of windows, doors or built-in features.

Compare apartments and verify the details

When comparing homes, use the same routine in each walkthrough. Look beyond total floor area: compare room shapes, circulation, storage, privacy and the furniture you need. Note questions to discuss with the developer or agent.

  • What are the internal dimensions and ceiling heights of each room?
  • Are walls, doors, windows and built-in elements shown in their confirmed positions?
  • Are the furniture sizes representative, and is there enough clearance around them?
  • Which details are illustrative, and which come from approved project plans?

A walkthrough is a visual aid, and accuracy depends on the source plans and model. Confirm measurements, specifications and any changes with the developer or a qualified professional before making commitments. Use approved plans and contract documents for definitive details.
